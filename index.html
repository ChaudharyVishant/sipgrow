How a SIP calculator works

A SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) invests a fixed amount every month. Each instalment grows from the day it is invested, so early instalments earn the most. This calculator uses the standard future-value formula with monthly compounding, and the yearly step-up raises your instalment by a set percentage each year.

Example: ₹10,000 a month for 15 years

Say you invest ₹10,000 every month for 15 years and your money grows at 12% a year. You put in ₹18 lakh in total. At the end, the estimated value is about ₹50 lakh, so roughly ₹32 lakh comes from growth. That growth is compounding: your gains also start earning gains. Try the same numbers above, then add a 10% yearly step-up and watch the final value jump.

The formula in simple words

Each month, the calculator adds your instalment to the balance and then applies one month of growth (yearly return divided by 12). It repeats this for every month of your plan. With a step-up, your instalment becomes bigger at the start of each new year.

Tips to get more from your SIP

Start early, because time matters more than amount. Keep investing when markets fall, since you buy more units at lower prices. Raise your SIP whenever your income rises. Check the plan once or twice a year, not every day. You can also record your own SIPs on our SIP Tracker page.

Frequently asked questions

What return rate should I enter?

Nobody can predict returns. Use a cautious number and test a few: try 8%, 10% and 12% to see the range.

What is a step-up SIP?

You increase your monthly amount by a fixed percentage every year, for example 10%, so your investment keeps pace with your income.

Are these results guaranteed?

No. Market-linked returns vary. The results are estimates for planning, not financial advice.

SIP or lumpsum: which is better?

A SIP suits people who earn monthly, because it spreads your buying across many months. A lumpsum suits money you already have. Many people do both.

Does this calculator include tax or fund charges?

No. Tax and expense charges reduce your real return, so choose a slightly lower return rate to stay safe.
