How a lumpsum calculator works

A lumpsum investment means you invest one big amount at once, instead of a small amount every month like a SIP. The money then grows with compounding: each year, your gains also start earning returns. This calculator uses the standard formula: final value = amount × (1 + yearly return) raised to the number of years.

Example: ₹1,00,000 for 10 years

If you invest ₹1,00,000 once and it grows at 12% a year, after 10 years it becomes about ₹3.1 lakh. Your gain is about ₹2.1 lakh. After 20 years the same amount becomes about ₹9.6 lakh. Waiting longer matters more than most people expect.

Lumpsum or SIP?

A lumpsum works when you already have a large amount, for example a bonus or savings. A SIP works when you invest from your monthly income. Markets go up and down, so a lumpsum made at a market peak can feel painful for a while. Many people invest a lumpsum in parts over a few months. You can compare both with our SIP calculator.

Frequently asked questions

What return should I enter?

Nobody can predict returns. Try a few cautious numbers such as 8%, 10% and 12% to see a range.

Are the results guaranteed?

No. Market-linked returns change every year. These are estimates for planning, not financial advice.

Does it include tax and fund charges?

No. Tax and expense charges lower your real return, so choose a slightly lower return rate to be safe.
